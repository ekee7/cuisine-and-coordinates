## Cuisine and Coordinates: Exploring and Predicting NYC Restaurant Inspection Outcomes

### Research Question

How do restaurant cuisine type and geographic location relate to health inspection outcomes in New York City, and can inspection scores be predicted using these characteristics?

### Dataset

We used the NYC Restaurant Inspection dataset from NYC Open Data, published by the New York City Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOHMH). The dataset contains over 296,000 restaurant inspection records, with each row representing an individual inspection and including restaurant characteristics, inspection details, and violation information.

### Approach

Our target variable was the inspection score, a numerical measure of violations identified during an inspection, where lower scores generally indicate better inspection outcomes. We focused on geographic features—including borough, ZIP code, latitude, and longitude—and restaurant cuisine type to investigate their relationships with inspection scores and build a predictive model.

Through feature engineering and regression modeling, we aimed to evaluate how well these restaurant characteristics explain variation in inspection scores across New York City.
